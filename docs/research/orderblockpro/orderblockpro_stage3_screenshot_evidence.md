## Strongest recurring evidence

### 1. Major Order Blocks / Icebergs behave as zones, not single-price lines
The highlighted red and green structures repeatedly occupy wide vertical price bands. Price often penetrates into the structure before rejecting or rotating.

Observed examples:
- Upper red sell-side block near 29514–29526 on 8 Sep: price trades into the block, then rotates sharply lower.
- Upper red sell-side block near 29614–29626 on 8 Sep: price enters the block, then sells off toward the lower green area.
- Upper yellow/red sell-side confluence near 29408–29420 on 16 Sep: price spikes into the stacked zone and reverses.

**Interpretation:** execution logic should treat these as spatial zones with internal hierarchy, not exact horizontal lines.

### 2. Confluence / stacking is a major part of the visual method
The user-provided yellow circles/boxes repeatedly emphasize **overlapping structures**, not isolated ones.

Typical stacks include:
- major red sell block + smaller nested sell structure,
- major green buy block + blue/cyan buy-liquidity structures,
- red sell block + yellow Fortress / mini-iceberg,
- green buy block + blue buy Fortress.

**Interpretation:** the tutorials appear to place higher informational value on clustered structures where several order-flow objects overlap.

### 3. Buy-side reactions and sell-side reactions are shown symmetrically
The screenshots contain repeated examples of both directions:

**Sell-side reaction pattern**
1. Price rallies into a red / magenta / yellow overhead cluster.
2. Price stalls, wicks, or briefly penetrates the zone.
3. Price rotates sharply lower.

**Buy-side reaction pattern**
1. Price sells into a green / blue / cyan lower cluster.
2. Price stabilizes or briefly undercuts the zone.
3. Price expands higher.

This strongly supports the earlier conclusion that OrderBlockPro is teaching a two-sided reaction framework rather than a long-only or short-only signal system.

### 4. Opposing zones appear to function as natural destination / target areas
Several annotated examples show movement from one structurally important cluster toward the next opposing cluster.

Examples:
- 2 Sep: a circled lower green buy-side area is followed by a rally toward an upper red sell-side block.
- 16 Sep: a lower blue/green buy-side cluster is followed by a strong rally into the large upper red sell block.
- 8 Sep: rejection from the upper red block travels toward lower green/blue support-side structures.

**Working hypothesis pending transcript verification:** the next opposing high-grade block/liquidity cluster may be used as a logical objective or target.

### 5. Fortresses / Mini Icebergs are visibly distinct and often nested
The legend shows:
- **Buy Fortress:** blue
- **Sell Fortress:** yellow

The 16 Sep screenshots are especially useful:
- a **yellow sell Fortress** is visibly stacked inside/under an overhead red sell-side structure before a rejection;
- a **blue buy Fortress** is visibly embedded in a lower support-side cluster before a rally.

This is important because Fortresses appear to be more than decoration—they are distinct structural objects that may materially strengthen a broader zone.

### 6. Minor Liquidity Levels appear to refine larger zones
Blue/cyan and purple/pink minor-liquidity structures frequently sit:
- inside major blocks,
- immediately above/below them,
- or between opposing major blocks.

This suggests a possible hierarchy:
- **Major block = broader structural area**
- **Minor liquidity = internal refinement / nearby reaction layer**
- **Fortress = concentrated mini-iceberg**
- **Nano-block = short-duration price-rip structure**

The exact execution priority still requires the narration.

## Refined working model

The screenshots support a stronger preliminary model:

1. **Identify the highest-grade major order block / iceberg zone.**
2. **Inspect whether minor liquidity, Fortresses, Nano-blocks, or contextual levels overlap it.**
3. **Treat stacked confluence as more important than a lone level.**
4. **Watch price behavior as it enters the zone rather than assuming the first boundary is the exact reversal price.**
5. **Use the next opposing structural cluster as a likely reaction/destination area.**
6. **Integrate contextual references (VWAP, value area, POC, previous-day/session levels, POWERLINE, KEY LEVEL, CLOSE) rather than reading the order-flow objects alone.**

This remains a **visual reconstruction**, not yet an authoritative transcription of OrderBlockPro's exact trade rules.
